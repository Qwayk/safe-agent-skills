# What you can ask the Fortnox skill to do

If you are not sure what to ask first, start with something you already recognize in Fortnox: your company details, a few customers, recent invoices, supplier bills, or stock balances. You do not need to learn command syntax before asking for those jobs in normal language.

## Good first asks

- "Show me our company details, recent customers, and the latest invoices waiting for payment."
- "List the suppliers we use most and compare them with the newest supplier invoices."
- "Show which articles are low on stock and which stock points they belong to."
- "Find projects or cost centers that look inactive or inconsistent."
- "Check payroll or time registrations for one employee and tell me if anything looks unusual."

## Everyday Fortnox work this helps with

- Follow sales and invoice work without jumping through several Fortnox views.
- Compare supplier records, supplier invoices, and payment status before bookkeeping changes.
- Review prices, articles, warehouse documents, and stock levels before product or stock updates.
- Spot bookkeeping or reporting issues in accounts, vouchers, periods, and supporting documents.
- Prepare a careful change when you really do want to create, update, bookkeep, send, or remove something.

## What you should expect back from the agent

- A short explanation of what it found in Fortnox.
- A list, summary, or export-ready result for the records you asked about.
- A reviewable plan before any risky change.
- A receipt and follow-up check after a real apply.

## Why this is better than a typical no-code automation

- It can look across existing Fortnox records instead of waiting for one new event.
- It can prepare bulk cleanups and bookkeeping changes for review before the account is changed.
- It stays explicit about risky actions instead of hiding them behind a generic API call.
- It leaves proof behind so you can check what changed later.
